Gmail buries your templates. Rico is a search box for them.

Gmail's Templates menu is three clicks deep and shows a flat list. Past ten templates you cannot find the one you want. Past twenty, you stop using the feature.

  1. Start a reply — Rico only wakes up inside a compose window.
  2. Press Ctrl+Shift+K — the palette opens over the compose box.
  3. Type a few letters — "fo" finds "Follow up", and ranks it above everything else that happens to contain an f and an o.
  4. Press Enter — the text lands at your cursor, line breaks intact, and Gmail's autosave picks it up as though you typed it.
🔍

Search that actually narrows

Fuzzy matching on titles, literal matching inside the text. Forgotten the name? Type "refund window" and it finds the one that says it.

👤

{FirstName}, filled in

Taken from whoever is in the To: field. Any other token you invent — {Company}, {Invoice} — asks you before it inserts, so no half-finished greeting goes out.

🐕

It notices what you retype

Write the same paragraph twice and Rico offers to keep it, with a title and tag already filled in. It stores a one-way hash, never your text.

⌨️

Not ⌘K, on purpose

Gmail already uses that for Insert Link and Chrome reserves it for the address bar. Rico stays off it — and lets you take it anyway if you never insert links.

Nothing leaves your browser.

No account, no server, no analytics. Snippets live in your own browser and are carried between your machines by Chrome's own sync. Rico reads the compose window you are typing in — inserting at your cursor is not possible otherwise — and the To: field for {FirstName}. Neither is stored or sent anywhere. It never touches your inbox or your sent mail. The only permission it asks for is storage.
